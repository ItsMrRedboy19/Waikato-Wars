Summary of the New Zealand Wars

The New Zealand Wars (Māori: Ngā pakanga o Aotearoa) were between 1845 and 1872, and involved wars between the New Zealand colonial government and its allies (e.g., Māori supporters) and opposing Māori groups, at times in league with settlers. The wars initially were about land sales, expanding very extensively by 1860 with Māori opposition to further loss of land and Crown sovereignty. Principal campaigns were directed against the Kīngitanga (Māori King) movement and Pai Mārire, a religious and political Māori organization fighting land conquest. During the 1860s, combat peaked with 18,000 British troops, supported by militia and artillery, against some 4,000 Māori warriors. Māori, though outnumbered, employed state-of-the-art defenses like anti-artillery bunkers and strategically located fortified pā. Guerrilla warfare also became a feature in later stages. Casualties were approximately 2,100 Māori and 800 Europeans. Major conflicts were the Waikato invasion (1863–64) and fighting with rangatiras like Riwha Tītokowaru and Te Kooti Arikirangi Te Turuki (1868–72). Colonial leaders raised local forces like militia, Forest Rangers, and kūpapa (Māori allied to the government), along with Australian volunteers. Land confiscation acts imposed on the Māori encouraged resistance, which further fueled tensions. Previously known as the Land Wars or Māori Wars, it was popularized by historians James Cowan and later James Belich. The wars are a significant page in New Zealand's history, intricately intertwined with issues of sovereignty, identity, and land.

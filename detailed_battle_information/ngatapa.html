Siege of Ngatapa

Ngatapa pā, a stronghold surrounded by cliffs and dense bush, was fortified by Te Kooti. Initial attacks by Ropata Wahawaha's forces in December 1868 failed due to a lack of resources. Te Kooti's subsequent raid on Tūranga for supplies also proved unsuccessful. Colonel Whitmore renewed the campaign against Te Kooti, amassing a 600-strong force comprising Armed Constabulary, Te Arawa, and Ngāti Porou fighters. The siege began on 31 December 1868, encircling Ngatapa and cutting off its water supply. Heavy rain and relentless attacks forced Te Kooti to abandon the pā on 5 January 1869, escaping with his followers down a steep cliff. The aftermath was grim: 130 male prisoners were executed, while women and children were taken captive. Government losses were minimal compared to Te Kooti's, who lost half his fighting force.

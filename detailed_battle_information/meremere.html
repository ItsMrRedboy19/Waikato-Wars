Withdraw from Meremere Pā

General Cameron established about 20 stockades and redoubts across the district to protect supply lines and block Māori advances, allocating nearly 6,000 men for this purpose. Despite this, Māori raids persisted, with notable attacks at Pukekohe East and Paerata in September. The situation shifted in late October when Waikato militia replaced regular troops at outposts, reinforcements arrived from Australia, and a second river steamer, HMS Curacoa, joined the campaign. On 31 October, a river flotilla bypassed Meremere fortifications, landing troops behind the defensive line. However, Māori forces evacuated Meremere and retreated across lagoons to their next defense at Rangiriri.

Waitangi Tribunal

In 1927, the Royal Commission on Confiscated Land found the Waikato land confiscations were excessive. The Waitangi Tribunal later concluded that Waikato–Tainui were forced into a defensive war rather than rebelling. In the 1990s, Waikato–Tainui bypassed the Tribunal to directly negotiate a treaty claims settlement with the Crown. In 1995, the Crown signed a settlement, providing cash and land valued at $170 million, admitting the unjust confiscation of land, apologizing for the invasion, and committing to return as much land as possible. Queen Elizabeth II endorsed the Crown's apology by signing the Waikato Raupatu Claims Settlement Act, marking efforts toward reconciliation and cooperation.

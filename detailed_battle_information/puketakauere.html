Battle of Puketakauere

The Battle of Pukekauere, fought on 27 June 1860, was a significant encounter during the First Taranaki War in New Zealand. This conflict arose from disputes over land ownership and sovereignty, particularly the controversial sale of the Waitara Block. Māori forces, led by Te Ātiawa chief Hapurona and supported by Ngāti Maniapoto, fortified two pā sites—Pukekauere and Onukukaitara—near Waitara. These fortifications were strategically designed to counter British artillery and tactics, incorporating rifle pits and underground shelters. The British, under Major Thomas Nelson, launched an attack on the pā, focusing their bombardment on Onukukaitara, which served as a decoy. However, the Māori defenders effectively utilized their fortifications, inflicting heavy casualties on the British forces. The battle resulted in a decisive Māori victory, with the British suffering 32 fatalities and numerous injuries, while Māori losses were minimal.

Waitangi Tribunal

In its 1996 report to the Government on Taranaki land claims, the Waitangi Tribunal observed that the war was begun by the Government, which had been the aggressor and unlawful in its actions in launching an attack by its armed forces. An opinion sought by the tribunal from a senior constitutional lawyer stated that the Governor, Thomas Gore Browne, and certain officers were liable for criminal and civil charges for their actions. The term "First Taranaki War" is opposed by some historians, who refer only to the Taranaki Wars, rejecting suggestions that post-1861 conflict was a second war. The 1927 Royal Commission on Confiscated Land also referred to the hostilities between 1864 and 1866 as a continuation of the initial Taranaki war.

Battle of Te Pōrere

Initially, Te Kooti launched a campaign in Taupō but faced overwhelming opposition from a government force largely composed of Māori allies (kupapa). His early attacks, including a strike on a Ngāti Kahungunu contingent at Tauranga-Taupō, forced him to retreat after suffering losses. Rewi Maniapoto, initially an ally, abandoned Te Kooti after witnessing poor performance in battle and the killing of Waikato-affiliated scouts. Te Kooti later suffered another defeat at Tokaanu, losing key warriors and retreating under heavy fire. The most crushing blow came at Te Pōrere, where Te Kooti constructed a European-style redoubt to prepare for a fixed battle—a deviation from his usual guerrilla tactics. Government forces, led by Major Thomas McDonnell and Te Keepa Te Rangihiwinui (Major Kemp), stormed the fort on 4 October 1869. Due to a design flaw in the redoubt that limited defensive fire, Te Kooti's forces suffered heavy casualties, including the loss of a close relative. He himself was wounded in battle. With the defeat at Te Pōrere marking the last major battle of the New Zealand Wars, his ability to wage open conflict was effectively ended. Horonuku, a key figure sheltering nearby, soon surrendered, solidifying the government’s victory.

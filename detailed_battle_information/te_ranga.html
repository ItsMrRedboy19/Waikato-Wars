Battle of Te Ranga

The Battle of Te Ranga on 21 June 1864 was a critical confrontation in the Tauranga campaign, following the defeat of British forces at Gate Pā. Kingite forces, including reinforcements from Ngāti Pikiao and Ngāti Porou, chose Te Ranga, a ridge near Gate Pā, to fortify and challenge the British troops. Colonel Greer led a British reconnaissance force of 600 men, later reinforced by 220 troops with cavalry and artillery. Fearing the Māori defenses would become stronger with time, Greer launched an immediate attack, culminating in fierce hand-to-hand combat. The British bayonets overwhelmed the Māori fighters, resulting in 83–120 Māori deaths, including the commander, Rawiri Puhirake. British losses included 13 dead and 39 wounded. Te Ranga was hailed as the British army's redemption after Gate Pā, but General Cameron expressed discontent with the war's continuation, admiring the Māori courage and chivalry. The victory marked the end of the Tauranga campaign. By late July, 133 Ngāi Te Rangi warriors surrendered, followed by most of the tribe in August. They ceded 50,000 acres of land and firearms to the British but retained some weapons, concluding one of the most significant phases of the New Zealand Wars.

Waitangi Tribunal

In 1927, the Royal Commission on Confiscated Land found the Waikato land confiscations were excessive. The Waitangi Tribunal later concluded that Waikato–Tainui were forced into a defensive war rather than rebelling. In the 1990s, Waikato–Tainui bypassed the Tribunal to directly negotiate a treaty claims settlement with the Crown. In 1995, the Crown signed a settlement, providing cash and land valued at $170 million, admitting the unjust confiscation of land, apologizing for the invasion, and committing to return as much land as possible. Queen Elizabeth II endorsed the Crown's apology by signing the Waikato Raupatu Claims Settlement Act, marking efforts toward reconciliation and cooperation.

The Fight at Rangiaowhia

On the morning of 21 February 1864, a force comprising British regular infantry and two colonial units, Captain Gustavus von Tempsky’s Forest Rangers and Colonel Marmaduke Nixon’s cavalry, attacked Rangiaowhia. The few defenders of the kāinga fought back. Houses were set on fire, with defenders shot as they tried to escape. When news of Rangiaowhia reached Pāterangi, the pā was abandoned, allowing Cameron’s army to occupy the fortifications unopposed. Kīngitanga forces then sought to establish a defensive line along the Hairini ridge in front of Rangiaowhia. Cameron rushed troops to engage Māori at Hairini, forcing their further retreat.

Invasion of the Waikato

Date: 12 July 1863 - April 1864

Location: Waikato, New Zealand

Result: British victory

Belligerents

United Kindom: Colony of New Zealand

Kīngitanga, North Island allies

Commanders and leaders

British: Duncan Cameron

Māori: Rewi Maniapoto, Wiremu Tamihana, Tāwhiao

Strength

United Kingdom: 14,000 British and colonial troops, several hundred British-allied Māori troops

Māori: ~4,000 troops, including 170 from Ngāi Tūhoe allies

Casualties and losses

United Kingdom: 700

Māori: 1,000, plus 80 taken prisoner

How it has been Redressed

In the modern day, the decendants of Ngāti Apakura, Ngāti Hinetū, representatives from the Catholic and Anglican Churches, school children, local body, and government officials, all gather together to pray and commemorate the invasion of Rangiaowhia in 1864. The commemoration was attended by hundreds of students from St Columba’s, Frankton; St Peter’s Catholic School, Cambridge; St Patrick’s, Te Awamutu; Bishop Edward Gaines School, Tokoroa; Marian Catholic School, Hamilton; Sacred Heart Catholic Girls’ College, Hamilton; St John’s Catholic College, Hamilton.

Invasion of Urewera

Following reports of Te Kooti’s March raids near Whakatāne, Colonel George Whitmore abandoned pursuit of Tītokowaru to lead an invasion into the Urewera mountains. His goal was to capture Te Kooti and destroy Tūhoe settlements to prevent them from sheltering him. Whitmore coordinated a three-pronged attack targeting Ruatāhuna, with forces advancing from the south, north, and west. The campaign began with an attack on Te Harema pā, where defenders were killed and women and children taken prisoner. Ruatāhuna settlements were systematically destroyed, but government forces withdrew after resistance and disease took their toll. Despite the costly and controversial expedition, the scorched earth policy succeeded in turning Tūhoe chiefs against Te Kooti, forcing him to flee. Te Kooti then moved towards Taupō, raiding a militia encampment at Opepe and killing nine soldiers while seizing supplies. He destroyed settlements that had refused him refuge before arriving at Tūrangi, Lake Taupō. By July, he led a large group towards Tokangamutu, hoping to forge an alliance with or overthrow Māori King Tāwhiao.

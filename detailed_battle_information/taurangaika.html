Battle of Taurangaika

Following the Moturoa defeat, Colonel Whitmore fortified his position at Nukumaru to protect Wanganui's settlements. Tītokowaru responded by constructing a highly advanced pā at Tauranga-ika with formidable defenses. From this base, he launched raids, including an assault on Weraroa Redoubt, forcing its abandonment. While tensions escalated, Whitmore shifted focus to defeating Te Kooti on the East Coast, leaving Wanganui temporarily. During Whitmore's absence, Tītokowaru demonstrated dominance over the Wanganui hinterland, refining Tauranga-ika into one of the most sophisticated pā of the New Zealand Wars, complete with trenches, stockades, rifle pits, and elevated firing positions. Whitmore returned in January 1869 and launched an offensive against Tauranga-ika in February, only to discover it abandoned. Tītokowaru's forces dispersed, reportedly due to a loss of mana tapu caused by personal scandal, leading to the rapid decline of his resistance. Whitmore pursued the scattered warriors, offering bounties and engaging in brutal reprisals. By March 1869, Tītokowaru's followers were either captured or surrendered in harsh conditions, effectively ending the war. Tītokowaru evaded capture, retreating to Kawau Pa, marking the conclusion of the conflict in South Taranaki.

Tītokowaru's War

Date: June 1868 - March 1869

Location: Taranaki, New Zealand

Result: Ngāti Ruanui and Ngaruahine withdrawal

Belligerents

United Kingdom:Colony of New Zealand, Māori allies

Ngāti Ruanui, Ngāruahine

Strength

British: 1250

Māori: 150 to 400

How it has been Redressed

Today, Taranaki iwi have still not received the land that was promised after the war. Some Taranaki women still wear the raukura in their hair, a sign of peace that originates from the Parihaka peaceful protests. The people still have not recovered the quality of life they had before the Government destroyed their communities. The families have not forgotten all the terrible things that happened to their people. Parents told their children what happened, and when those children grew up, they told their children, and so on till the present day.

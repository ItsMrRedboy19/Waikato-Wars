Hōne Heke's Protest

Hōne Heke Pōkai, a Ngāpuhi chief, was a proud leader. He had been the first chief to sign the treaty, hoping it would benefit his people. However, many Ngāpuhi faced hard times after 1840, especially when the capital was moved from the Bay of Islands to Auckland in 1841. This meant there was less trading in the north. In protest, Heke Pōkai chopped down the British flagpole at Kororāreka (which the British called Russell) not once but three times between July 1844 and January 1845. So Governor FitzRoy brought troops from Sydney and stationed them in the town. The Northern War began on 11 March 1845 when Heke Pōkai and his ally, Kawiti, attacked and routed the British garrison at Kororāreka. Most of the town was destroyed during the fighting. Heke Pōkai’s seemingly easy victory shocked Pākehā. They had expected the British soldiers, with their superior weapons, to be more than a match for the Māori warriors and their antique muskets.

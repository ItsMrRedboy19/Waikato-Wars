Siege of Waerenga-a-Hika

In late October 1865, Donald McLean orchestrated an expedition against the Pai Mārire community at Waerenga-a-Hika pā near Turanga (modern-day Gisborne). Several hundred Māori sought refuge in the pā amidst the East Coast wars. McLean assembled 300 Ngāti Porou volunteers and a mixed force of European troops, delivering an ultimatum that demanded allegiance to the Queen, surrender of arms, and expulsion of outsiders. When terms were largely ignored, the government forces initiated a seven-day siege on 16 November. The pā had strong defenses including stockades and earthworks. The siege involved rifle fire and artillery bombardments, resulting in over 100 Pai Mārire deaths and 100 wounded. Government losses totaled 11 dead and 20 wounded. On 22 November, after artillery attacks demoralized the garrison, 400 occupants surrendered and were either released or imprisoned on the Chatham Islands. Among the prisoners was Te Kooti, a future Māori guerrilla leader, who was suspected of collaboration but later released. The pā was destroyed, and the siege marked significant losses for the Pai Mārire community.

East Cape War

Date: 13 April 1865 to 12 October 1866

Location: East Coast, New Zealand

Belligerents

United Kingdom: Colony of New Zealand

Māori: Whakatohea Māori, Urewera Māori, Ngai Tama Māori

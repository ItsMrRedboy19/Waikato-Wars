Battle of Moturoa

In September 1868, Tītokowaru strengthened his force by gaining the support of several hapū, doubling his strength to about 150-200 warriors. By early October, he established a pā at Otoia, northeast of Patea, preparing for conflict by repairing weapons and gathering supplies. His raids on settlers aimed to provoke the government into engaging at his pā, but without success. Colonel George S. Whitmore replaced McDonnell as the commander and moved his field headquarters to Wairoa (modern-day Waverley) to counter the perceived threat from Tītokowaru's southward movements. On the night of 6 November 1868, Whitmore led an attacking column of 550 troops, including Wanganui kupapa, toward Moturoa pā. However, due to strong defensive fortifications and strategic positions established by Tītokowaru, the colonial forces suffered heavy losses in the assault on 7 November, losing 19 killed or missing and 20 wounded. In contrast, only one Māori defender was killed. The defeat exposed flaws in Whitmore's strategy and marked a significant setback for the colonial troops. Despite criticism, historians have noted Whitmore's underestimation of the pā's defenses and acknowledged Tītokowaru's excellence as a commander.

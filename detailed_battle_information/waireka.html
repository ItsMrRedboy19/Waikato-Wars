Battle of Waireka

Towards the end of the 1850s, New Plymouth settlers put pressure on Governor Browne to make more land available. The governor bought the Waitara block from the young Te Āti Awa chief Te Teira, even though Browne knew the other owners of the block opposed the sale. Expecting trouble, Browne sent troops to the area to build a fort. Wīremu Kīngi Te Rangitāke, a senior leader of Te Āti Awa, was outraged that the governor claimed to have bought his ancestral land and was intending it for Pākehā farms. In February 1860, he drove the surveyors away and built a pā at Te Kohia. This became the scene of the first battle of the Taranaki War on 17 March 1860, which was inconclusive. A British victory followed at the Battle of Waireka, fought on 28 March 1860, was a significant encounter during the Taranaki Wars in New Zealand. It took place near New Plymouth and involved British troops, local settlers, and Māori forces. The conflict arose from disputes over land ownership and sovereignty. The British aimed to rescue settlers trapped near the Waireka Stream, while Māori forces sought to defend their territory.

Date: 17 March 1840 ~ 18 March 1861

Location: Taranaki, New Zealand

Result: Inconclusive

Belligerents

United Kingdom: Colony of New Zealand

Waitangi Tribunal

In its 1996 report to the Government on Taranaki land claims, the Waitangi Tribunal observed that the war was begun by the Government, which had been the aggressor and unlawful in its actions in launching an attack by its armed forces. An opinion sought by the tribunal from a senior constitutional lawyer stated that the Governor, Thomas Gore Browne, and certain officers were liable for criminal and civil charges for their actions. The term "First Taranaki War" is opposed by some historians, who refer only to the Taranaki Wars, rejecting suggestions that post-1861 conflict was a second war. The 1927 Royal Commission on Confiscated Land also referred to the hostilities between 1864 and 1866 as a continuation of the initial Taranaki war.
